About Martin Harris

A Man of Faith and Conviction

Martin Harris (1783-1875) was a prosperous farmer and one of the Three Witnesses to the Book of Mormon, whose testimony has remained unwavering throughout history.

Born in Easton, New York, Martin Harris became a successful farmer and businessman in Palmyra. His life took a dramatic turn when he met Joseph Smith and became instrumental in the translation and publication of the Book of Mormon.

Despite facing ridicule, financial hardship, and persecution, Harris never denied his testimony of having seen the golden plates and an angel. His unwavering witness continues to inspire millions worldwide.

Timeline of His Life

1783

Birth in Easton, New York

Martin Harris was born on May 18, 1783, in Easton, New York, to Nathan and Rhoda Harris.

1808

Marriage and Settlement

Married Lucy Harris and established himself as a successful farmer in Palmyra, New York.

1827

Meets Joseph Smith

First encounters Joseph Smith and learns about the golden plates.

1828

Scribe for Translation

Serves as scribe for the translation of the Book of Mormon. The loss of 116 pages occurs.

1829

Witness to the Golden Plates

Becomes one of the Three Witnesses, testifying he saw the plates and an angel.

1830

Publication of the Book of Mormon

Mortgages his farm for $3,000 to finance the first printing of 5,000 copies.

1837

Separation from the Church

Becomes estranged from the main body of the Church during a period of apostasy.

1870

Journey to Utah

Travels to Utah Territory at age 87 to rejoin the Saints.

1875

Death and Testimony

Died in Clarkston, Utah, on July 10, 1875, never having denied his witness.

His Enduring Legacy

The Testimony

His written testimony, printed in every Book of Mormon, stands as an unshakable witness to millions of readers worldwide.

Historic Sites

The Martin Harris farm, cabin, and other historic locations serve as tangible connections to early Church history.

Faith Under Trial

His story demonstrates the power of maintaining faith through persecution, loss, and personal hardship.

Sacrifice

His willingness to sacrifice his wealth and reputation for the publication of the Book of Mormon exemplifies dedication.
