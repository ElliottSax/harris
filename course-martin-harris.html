Course Overview

Martin Harris (1783-1875) was far more than just a wealthy farmer who financed the Book of Mormon. This comprehensive course examines his life as a treasure seeker, one of the Three Witnesses to the Book of Mormon, and a spiritual seeker who moved between six different restoration churches.

His willingness to follow evidence, admit complexity, and speak honestly about his spiritual experiences—even when that honesty created problems for him—makes him a perfect patron for honest historical inquiry into the restoration movement.

What You'll Learn

  • Understand Martin Harris's role in early restoration history and his significance beyond being a financial backer
  • Analyze the cultural context of folk magic and treasure seeking in 1820s upstate New York
  • Examine the evolution of witness testimony across Harris's lifetime and what it reveals about sacred experience
  • Evaluate Harris's movement between different restoration churches and what it teaches about authority and conviction
  • Consider how Harris's honesty about spiritual experiences informs historical methodology
  • Apply critical thinking to primary source documents from Harris's life

Course Syllabus

  • Module 1: Early Life and Cultural Context (1783-1827)
    3 hours • 3 lessons

    Explore the Burned-Over District, folk magic practices, and Harris's prosperity and religious seeking before 1827.

  • Module 2: Meeting Joseph Smith (1827-1829)
    3 hours • 3 lessons

    The golden plates story, the lost 116 pages incident, and Harris's witness experience.

  • Module 3: Financing the Book of Mormon (1829-1830)
    2.5 hours • 3 lessons

    Harris's decision to mortgage his farm, the publishing process, and the consequences for his family.

  • Module 4: Excommunication and Kirtland Crisis (1831-1837)
    3 hours • 3 lessons

    Early church life, the Kirtland Safety Society collapse, and Harris's public criticism and excommunication.

  • Module 5: Wandering Between Churches (1838-1869)
    3.5 hours • 3 lessons

    The Strangite movement, other restoration groups, and Harris's continuing testimony across church affiliations.

  • Module 6: The Evolving Testimony
    3.5 hours • 3 lessons

    Physical vs. spiritual sight, studying changing accounts, and understanding sacred experience.

  • Module 7: Journey to Utah (1870-1875)
    2.5 hours • 3 lessons

    The invitation west, final years in Utah, death and legacy.

  • Module 8: Martin Harris and Historical Methodology
    3 hours • 3 lessons

    Harris as historical case study, working with witness testimony, and why we study Martin Harris.

Your Instructor

Dr. Rebecca Winters

Ph.D. in American Religious History, Yale University

Dr. Winters specializes in early American folk religion and the restoration movement. She has published extensively on the Three Witnesses and treasure-seeking culture in upstate New York. Her work focuses on the intersection of magical worldviews and religious experience in 19th century America.
